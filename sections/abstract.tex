\begin{abstract}
The paired difference $D=A-B$ is a linearly sufficient feature for a binary outcome when the population coefficient is proportional to $(1,-1)$.
Under joint normality that linear condition is full sufficiency for $Y$.
The Paired Efficiency Factor $\eta$ does not decide that alignment.
It decides the variance of the difference relative to unpaired measurement.
Reliability is decided not by $\eta$ but by the ratio of its signal and noise components, $\eta_S/\eta_N$.
Under a correctly specified aligned Gaussian probit, the redundant second coefficient costs $1/(2n)$ nats of expected test log-loss, with a second-order remainder fitted on three aligned designs.
Alignment and reliability are second-moment identities.
The Gaussian reduction of log-loss, Brier score, AUC and mutual information to $(z,r^2)$ is not.
\end{abstract}
