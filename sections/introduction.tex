\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Fisher's Paired Efficiency}

Fisher \parencite{fisher1935} showed that pairing $n$ matched observations multiplies the variance of the mean difference by $(1-\rho)$ relative to two independent samples, where $\rho$ is the intra-pair correlation.
The resulting efficiency is $1/(1-\rho)$.
The formula assumes that the two members of each pair have the same marginal variance.

The companion empirical paper \parencite{brownPEFempirical} allows a variance ratio $\kappa = \sigma_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}/\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}$.
The Paired Efficiency Factor is
\begin{equation}
  \eta(\kappa,\rho)
  = \frac{1+\kappa}{1+\kappa-2\sqrt{\kappa}\,\rho}.
  \label{eq:pef_intro}
\end{equation}
At $\kappa = 1$ this is Fisher's formula.
In the sports tier of that paper, $\rho$ is the Pearson correlation of home and away values of one performance indicator across fixtures.
This note studies the formula.
It does not repeat the empirical design.

\subsection{One Scalar}
\label{sec:related}

Set $\tau = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\kappa$ and
\begin{equation}
  u = \rho\,\sech\tau = \frac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}\,\rho}{\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}} = \frac{2\sigma_{AB}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}}.
  \label{eq:u_def}
\end{equation}
Then $\eta = 1/(1-u)$ wherever the denominator of \cref{eq:pef_intro} is positive.
The same $u$ is the pairwise intraclass correlation of the two margins \parencite{fisher1925}.
Lin's concordance coefficient reduces to $u$ when the two means are equal, because the bias factor is then $C_b = \sech\tau$ \parencite{lin1989}.
The Pearson correlation of the two margins is $\rho$.
It is not $u$.

Classical paired-variance tests address a different question.
The Pitman--Morgan test compares $\sigma_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}$ with $\sigma_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}$ through the correlation of within-pair sums and differences \parencite{pitman1939,morgan1939}.
The distribution of a ratio of correlated sample variances is older still \parencite{bose1935}.
The Behrens--Fisher problem is for independent samples \parencite{welch1947}.
Under bivariate normality, \textcite{brownPEFempirical} identify $\eta$ with the Pitman efficiency of the paired $t$-test against the independent $t$-test \parencite{lehmann1999}.
That identification is empirical-owned.
What is added here is the sampling variance of the plug-in $\hat u$.

Several further readings of $u$ are classical once the scalar is named.
Any number in $(-1,1)$ is a cosine, so $\eta = 1/(1-\cos\sigma)$ is a latitude formula, not a map from $(\kappa,\rho)$ to a point on the sphere.
Any $u\in(0,1)$ is the success parameter of a geometric distribution.
The Fisher information of that latent family is a function of $u$ alone.
Its pull-back is not a metric on the $(\tau,\rho)$-plane.
These readings are collected as remarks in \cref{sec:scalar}.
They are not further parameters.

\subsection{Contributions}

The note has three results, and a set of remarks.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The canonical form $\eta = \cosh\tau/(\cosh\tau-\rho)$ and the involution $\kappa\leftrightarrow 1/\kappa$ (\cref{sec:canonical}).
  The involution fixes $\eta$.
  It does not fix comparisons that use the labelled margin $X_{\mathrm{A}}$.
  \item The identification $\eta = 1/(1-u)$ with the pairwise intraclass correlation (\cref{eq:u_def}).
  \item Under bivariate normality, $\Var(\hat u)$ depends on $\tau$ as well as on $u$ (\cref{prop:plugin}).
  No transform of $u$ stabilises the plug-in exactly.
  At $\tau = 0$ the stabilising transform is Fisher's $z = \arctanh\rho$ \parencite{fisher1921}, not $\psi = 2\arctanh\sqrt{\rho}$.
\end{enumerate}

\Cref{sec:numerical} checks the plug-in formula by simulation.
Algebraic identities are checked only as implementation tests.
They cannot falsify a closed form.

\subsection{Relation to the Empirical Companion}

The empirical paper estimates $(\kappa,\rho)$ and studies whether a relative feature helps match-outcome prediction.
It does not use the involution as a theorem, the coordinate $\psi$, the latent geometric family, or a regime change at $\rho = 0$.
This note uses that paper's paired estimates only as numerical checks of algebra.
The Gaussian mutual information $I(X;Y)$ in \textcite{brownPEFempirical} is between a relative feature and a binary outcome.
It depends on the mean separation.
It is not $\log\eta$, and it is not $-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1-\rho^{2})$.
The second of those quantities is the mutual information of the two Gaussian margins.
It does not depend on $\kappa$.
