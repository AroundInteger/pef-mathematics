\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fisher showed that pairing reduces the variance of a mean-difference estimator by the factor $(1-\rho)^{-1}$ when the two margins share a common variance~\parencite{fisher1935}.
The Paired Efficiency Factor extends that ratio to unequal margins~\parencite{brownPEFempirical}:
\begin{equation}
  \eta
  = \frac{1+\kappa}{1+\kappa-2\sqrt{\kappa}\,\rho},
  \label{eq:pef_intro}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa=\sigma_B^2/\sigma_A^2$ and $\rho=\Corr(A,B)$.
This paper asks four questions about~\cref{eq:pef_intro} and about the paired difference $D=A-B$ as a feature for a binary outcome.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item When is $D$ a linearly sufficient feature for a binary outcome?
  \item What does $\eta$ decide instead?
  \item What does the redundant coefficient cost in a finite sample?
  \item Which of those statements survive a non-Gaussian margin?
\end{enumerate}

The contribution is a separation of three quantities that are routinely conflated: efficiency ($\eta$), linear alignment (whether $D$ matches the pair in $R^2$), and reliability ($\eta_S/\eta_N$).
A quantified cost of ignoring alignment, and a map of which conclusions require Gaussian margins, complete the account.
Match-level measurements live in the empirical companion~\parencite{brownPEFempirical}.
