\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The latent model treats $S$ as persistent pair-level signal, $N$ as occasion-level measurement noise, $M$ as a continuous margin for the binary outcome, and $\varepsilon$ as residual margin noise.
For a team KPI, $A$ and $B$ are home and away counts in a match, $S$ is a team-strength component that recurs across fixtures, and $N$ absorbs match-to-match variation.
That reading motivates the reliability split $\eta_S/\eta_N$, but it is an interpretation of second moments, not a claim that the sports data were generated from this model.

The structure $S\to M$ is one-way.
In invasion games the match state can change how teams play, so KPIs may respond to the outcome as well as predict it.
Reverse causation of that kind lies outside the present account.

The paper separates efficiency, linear alignment and reliability.
It does not instruct an analyst which column to build.
Match-level gaps between $D$, $A$ and $(A,B)$ are measured in the empirical companion~\parencite{brownPEFempirical}.
Geometry of $\eta$ on the half-strip is reserved for a separate manuscript and is not used here.
