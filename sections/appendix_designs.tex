\section{Simulation designs}
\label{app:designs}

All numerical illustrations reuse the stored outputs of the \texttt{pef\_numerics} programme.
No new Monte Carlo was run for this draft.
Parameters below are taken from the experiment scripts.

\subsection*{Finite-sample cells (E3)}

The dimensionless base point is
\[
  z=0,\quad \rho_S=0.2,\quad \rho_N=0.6,\quad w=0.5,\quad q=0.8.
\]
Aligned designs: A1 is the base point; A2 uses $(z,\rho_S,\rho_N,w,q)=(0.5,-0.3,0.3,0.3,0.9)$; A3 uses $(0,0.5,0.8,0.8,0.6)$.
Training sizes are $n\in\{50,100,200,500,1000,2000,5000\}$ with $R=3000$ replicates per cell.
Expected test log-loss is computed exactly from the fitted probit, not from a held-out sample.

Cell~B sets the outcome coefficient ratio $b_B/b_A$ so that $\Delta_\infty=1/(2n^*)$ at nominal $n^*\in\{100,300,1000\}$.
Cell~C sets $\kappa_N$ for $\Delta_\infty=1/(2\cdot 300)$ at $b=c$.
Empirical $n^*$ is the interpolated zero-crossing of the Monte Carlo gain curve, with the intervals reported in~\cref{tab:crossover}.

\subsection*{Counterexample designs (Table~\ref{tab:eta2})}

The three $(\Sigma_S,\Sigma_N,b)$ triples are stored exactly in \texttt{E5\_eta2\_counterexample.csv}.
They are algebraic, not simulated.
Every case has $\eta(\Sigma_K)=2$.

\subsection*{Stress battery (E5)}

Two design points share the E3 base second moments: an aligned point, and a misaligned point with $b_B/b_A\approx 1.177$ (the coefficient asymmetry whose probit crossover is $n^*=300$).
Each law uses $40$ replicates of $n=2500$ draws.
Laws: Gaussian; multivariate $t$ with $3$ and $5$ degrees of freedom; Gaussian copula with gamma margins; bivariate Poisson; an ordinal cut of a latent Gaussian; five per cent contamination; and a repeated-team law that is marginally Gaussian with club-level dependence.

\subsection*{Repeated-team estimator}

Truth: $\tau^2=1$, $\lambda=0.5$, residual variances $(1,1.44)$, residual correlation $0.4$, giving $\eta_S/\eta_N\approx 0.337$.
Estimation is Gaussian maximum likelihood for $(\mu_A,\mu_B,\tau^2,\lambda,\Sigma_\varepsilon)$ with a Woodbury likelihood checked against a dense covariance.
Wald intervals use the observed information at nominal level $95\%$.
Rugby-sized design: $16$ teams, $240$ matches, $60$ replicates.
Football-sized design: $24$ teams, $1{,}104$ matches, $30$ replicates.
